# Caption Emojis are compiled vector art in one Emoji Layer, anchored by Word Key and tagged at selection preparation

**Status:** accepted by the owner, 2026-11-26, with the Add Emojis specification (`.scratch/add-emojis/spec.md`).

The earlier emoji feature took emojis from a 90-word English keyword table, drew 29 Noto raster sprites through an FFmpeg overlay chain and a CSS sprite sheet, and worked only on the 24 classic presets. Narriflow now has its own emoji pack (1,008 single designs, 400 phrase badges, skin-tone variants; four more single designs were drawn for it later in the pack's style) and 84 Scene Styles that move, stack and split their words. The new feature must draw that art in the template's colours, the same in the Studio and the export, for every preset kind and in both directions, with AI choosing the words in any language. libass cannot draw colour fonts (issue 381; the colour-font pull requests closed unmerged), but it draws ASS vector drawings (`\p1`), which the Scene writer already emits.

## Decision

- **Own art, compiled once to flat fills.** `packages/caption-emoji` compiles every SVG into absolute non-zero `M/L/C/Z` fill layers on the 64 grid: transforms baked, strokes expanded, badge clips applied, painter order kept. A layer's colour is a palette role (`a`, `b`, `c`), the fixed ink, skin, a literal, or a per-tone colour where the pack redraws dark-tone faces. The sticker outline is a precompiled fill. Under Match template every design takes the template palette, round faces included: there is no face exception (the owner chose this on 2026-11-26 after yellow faces sat beside red cats); a named palette or Custom applies as given. A pixel check against the source SVGs gates the build. Both renderers then draw emojis with the shape support they already have. Raster sprites, a colour font and a second FFmpeg input were rejected.
- **One Emoji Layer, built from cue boxes outside the scene build.** `buildCaptionEmojiPieces` in `packages/caption-scene/src/emoji/` places and draws every emoji from cue boxes. The classic line, the Caption Block and the Caption Scene differ only in how they make those boxes (`classicLineCueBox`, `captionBlockCueBox`, `captionSceneEmojiCueBoxes`), and a Scene Style that throws gives the classic box. Emojis are not in `SceneCue` or `sceneBuildKey`, so an emoji edit rebuilds a template's scene only when it changes a cue's hero (the word under a drawn emoji is its cue's hero). Placement, size, timing and the style derivation (`captionEmojiLayerStyle`) are shared, so no default lives in `apps/`.
- **Close to what is on screen when it pops, at full strength for 250 ms.** An auto emoji tries above its cue (full size, then 0.7), then level with it at its reading end and its other end, and below last; when above would put its top in the face zone (the top 30% of the frame), below comes first and above last. Explicit Above and Below and a style's `beside-end` or `below` slot keep their order. Every slot must sit within half the emoji's height of the cue's text that reads on the emoji's first painted frame, after any wait for its slot (`CAPTION_EMOJI_MAX_GAP`): a Scene Style's cue box is one late sample that holds rows a stack reveals later, so its cue box also reports what is drawn at a time (`shownAt`), and so does a Dynamic block (each word from its onset). Only when no such slot is clear may it sit within half a height of the text that reads by the end of its pop and within one height of the first frame's (a bubble or frame that grows in with the word), rather than be dropped. Below sits under that text and must stay clear of the later rows on every frame. One static position per emoji, so the Studio and the export stay identical. The slot is the one the smallest Size choice finds (the face zone is judged at the default size); a larger choice draws there at the largest of its sizes that fits as well, else at the smaller choice's size, so for one cue Small ≤ Medium ≤ Large, on one side. Every drawn emoji shows at least 340 ms: 250 ms at full strength, then its 90 ms fade. A word that leaves less than that of its cue holds past it, never past the clip's end, and the next emoji of a cue starts when the latest one drawn ends, or draws nothing: two emojis of one cue never show at once. An emoji that would only fit further away is dropped: the owner rejected emojis "far from the captions" (ticket 33).
- **One drawing box per emoji, set by corner move points.** libass sizes and aligns a drawing by all its points, move points included. Every part of one emoji starts its path at the two corners of the same box, so `\pos` stays fixed and one `\fscx`/`\fscy` chain scales every part through the pop. The writer then emits one event per piece (at most 21 per emoji). The hard shadow is its own piece, because a shadow paint layer is offset by scale and would be cut per frame. The Scene writer and the Studio painter needed no change.
- **Anchors by Word Key, resolved in the plan by nearest source time.** `studioEdits.captionEmojis` maps a Word Key to an Emoji Ref. The plan resolves each entry to the kept word whose source start is nearest, within 400 ms and inside the key's utterance. Line rewrites, merges and trims therefore need no re-anchoring reducer; one would not know which line a whole-transcript edit changed. A cut, censored or out-of-range word draws nothing and keeps its entry.
- **Import tagging at selection preparation, frozen into the capsule.** When `addEmojis` is on, `clip-selection-preparation.ts` tags the source beside the AI filler tags, under the same deadline, and freezes the picks into the selection capsule (`editor.emojiPicks`, the first thing dropped when the capsule is too large). Replay reads the capsule and calls no model. Row building keeps each clip's picks on its kept words and applies density. The Studio suggest route and Apply to all call the same tagger (`tagCaptionEmojis`). A failed tag means no emojis, never a failed import.
- **The model answers every caption line, after it marks grave passages silent.** Code splits the transcript into caption lines (at most 4 words, ending early at punctuation) and gives each word that may carry an emoji a letter. Each request (up to 96 lines, with 8 lines of context each side) has its own strict schema, `{ silent: [{ from, to }], lines: { L0: string, …, Ln: string } }`, with every line required; a value is `"<letter> <emoji> <strength> <yes|no>"`, or `"X"`. The server maps the letter to its own word, drops a `no` (or an emoji on a negation word) unless the emoji itself pictures "no", drops lines inside a silent range and the 6 lines after one, retries a reply that answers fewer than half its lines, and asks once more when a reply silences more than half of them, keeping the one with fewer silent lines. A free list of picks let the model stop at about one per three lines; one required answer per line gets one on most lines (ticket 32). The prompt also says a word that rejects or reverses an idea never takes that idea's emoji, a discourse word (like, just, so) is never pictured, and danger takes ⚠️ or 🚨, 💀 only for death (ticket 34). A silent field was first added because an Urdu illness passage got emojis in 3 of 6 runs with the rule in the prompt alone, and 0 of 6 with the field.

## Consequences

Adding or redrawing an emoji is an art change and a rebuild of the generated data; neither renderer changes. The art is about 3.9 MB in 17 shards. The Studio loads shards on demand and the picker as a lazy chunk. An AI run loads its answer's art (up to 6 s) before it commits, and a glyph without art is an empty box, never a placeholder disc (ticket 34). The Worker bundles only the catalog index (about 118 kB). Placement is checked across all 84 Scene Styles on three canvases, demo and fast cues, and fails above a drop-rate threshold. On fast speech a style may drop an emoji that has no room, which is not an error. A new Scene Style must draw emojis (no opt-out); it may declare an `emojiSlot` when its shape needs another side. Emoji typed into caption text stays text, drawn by the single-colour fallback face, and subtitle files never contain added emojis.
