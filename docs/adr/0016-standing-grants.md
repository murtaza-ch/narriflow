# Standing Grants let a person authorize one Autopilot rule's output to go out unattended, and count their caps under the delivery's own lock

A Workspace Owner or Admin can give one Autopilot rule standing authority for its output to go out without a click: its proposed posts accepted into a Social Account's queue (`publish`), or its finished exports sent as a Review Round to named reviewers (`review_send`). Everything else about delivery stays as it was. The grant supplies the click and nothing more.

- **A grant is a row with an owner, caps, an expiry and a status.** `StandingGrant` holds the kind, the rule, the account or the reviewer list, `dailyCap`, `weeklyCap`, `expiresAt` (at most 90 days ahead), `status` (`active`, `paused`, `expired`, `revoked`), `pausedReason`, `revision` and `grantedByUserId`. `StandingGrantEvent` is its audit trail. Creating, renewing, pausing and revoking need `workspace.manage`, which only Owners and Admins hold; any member can read. TikTok accounts are refused, because TikTok needs the creator's choices on every post. A rule has at most one live grant per account and one for review rounds, enforced under the rule's row lock. Renewing restates the terms, bumps `revision`, resumes the grant, and makes the renewer the person it acts for.
- **Caps count what went out, over rolling windows, under the lock that serializes the delivery.** A post grant counts Social Posts that carry its ID and whose proposal was accepted in the last 24 hours and 7 days; a review grant counts Review Rounds it created. The count runs inside the delivery's own transaction, after the grant's row is locked: for a post, after the Social Account's row lock that already chooses the slot, so every acceptance for that account is serialized; for a round, in the round's serializable transaction, so a competing round fails with a serialization error and is retried. The caps are read from the locked row, not from the caller. A cancelled post still counts: the cap bounds what was authorized, not what survived.
- **Coverage is by the rule that froze the finishing, and by nothing else.** The Autopilot import writes `ContentPack.autopilotRuleId`; the handoff copies it to `GenerationFinishing.autopilotRuleId`; a request's finishing, REST or MCP, never sets it. Provenance of the Project (which rule imported it) would let anyone with `publishing:write` aim their own settings at a granted account, so it isn't used.
- **A grant is bound to the rule as its granter saw it.** `StandingGrant.ruleFingerprint` hashes the rule's source kind, feed URL or Zoom connection and topic filter, the Zoom connection's revoked flag, and title prefix, its Recipe's ID and content revision (which stands for the language, Content Pack and finishing settings, and moves only when those change, never on a rename), and the Brand Profile the Recipe resolves to, with that profile's `approvalRule` and deleted flag. Names do not count: renaming the rule or the Recipe changes nothing. `admit` recomputes it in the delivery's transaction and throws `standing_grant_rule_changed` on a mismatch; the transaction rolls back, so the caller pauses the grant (`rule_changed`) right after. Disconnecting Zoom pauses covering rules and their grants. Editing a rule, including pointing it at another Recipe, pauses its grants in the edit's transaction; so does a Recipe save that changes content (`onRecipeContentChanged`, for every rule using the Recipe). Lock order is the Recipe, then each rule in id order, then its grants. Renewing re-binds. A Brand Profile that stops requiring approval is a mismatch, so an Editor can't remove the gate by editing.
- **Auto-accepting is the ordinary accept, run for the granter.** After finishing proposes a post, it asks the grant module to accept it. That finds the active publish grant of the finishing's rule for that account, checks that the granter still holds `workspace.manage`, and calls `publishingQueue.accept` as the granter with the grant's `admit` hook. The hook is passed to the scheduling store, which runs it right after taking the account lock and writes the grant's ID and revision on the converted Social Post as its approval evidence. Export freshness and the Brand Profile approval gate run exactly as they do for a person, and the grant never passes an override reason, so an `approval_required` brand needs an approved round. Any refusal (cap, no slots, no approved round, a stale clip) leaves the proposal a proposal, and the pick records why. A grant that the granter's loss of role makes unusable is paused instead.
- **A review grant creates the round through the review service.** When finishing ends complete and its delivery is `send_review_round`, it asks the grant module to send. The round is created with `ReviewRound.finishingId`, which is unique, so a re-claim or a second worker finds the round it already made and sends nothing more. The plan check (`review.rooms`), the cap and the grant are checked in that order; a refusal ends the finishing `partial` with a `review_not_sent_…` reason and the clips ready to review. The round's reviewers are the grant's list, and whether approval is required follows the Project's Brand Profile.
- **Failures pause the grant where `social_post.finished` is derived.** `emitSocialPostFinished` is called as the last act of every transaction that settles a post. Before it flushes, it now pauses the grant of a post that ended `failed` or `needs_attention`, and queues `grant.paused`; the one flush then writes both events, counter last. The pause is conditional on the grant being active at the revision the post was accepted under, so a replay does nothing and a late outcome of a post from before a renewal doesn't pause the renewed grant. The pause sets a notice flag; the worker emails the granter (the Workspace owner if they no longer manage) once.
- **Expiry is a maintenance sweep.** The worker's maintenance loop moves active and paused grants past `expiresAt` to `expired`, one transaction each, with an audit line and a `grant.expired` event keyed by the grant and revision.
- **A grant that isn't active holds or returns its posts.** The publisher creates an attempt for a post that carries a grant only while that grant is active and unexpired, checked under a shared lock on the grant row taken after the post's lock. A paused grant therefore holds its posts, and renewing releases them. For a revoked or expired grant, `returnGrantPosts` reverts each post that has no attempt yet to the draft proposal it was (no time, no frozen state, no grant, acceptance cleared) and writes a `returned` audit line; the sweep repeats this, so an interrupted revoke finishes. A post with an attempt is left to finish. Reverting in place works because acceptance converts the same Social Post and keeps its `proposal-accept` key.
- **The grant outlives its rule and account.** The rule and account foreign keys are `SET NULL`, and deleting a rule, disconnecting an account, demoting or removing the granter, or editing the rule revoke or pause the grant in the transaction that does it. Lock order is rule, then grant, then the Workspace Event counter.
- **Reviewers can opt out.** A review email sent by a grant carries a signed link naming the Workspace and the address. The page asks first (mail scanners follow links), and its button records a `ReviewerOptOut` row. Grants skip recorded addresses; with none left the finishing ends `review_not_sent_opted_out`.
- **Events** `grant.paused` (`grantId`, `kind`, `autopilotRuleId`, `reason`) and `grant.expired` (`grantId`, `kind`, `autopilotRuleId`) join the feed ([ADR 0014](0014-workspace-events.md)). They carry no account, post or address, so they need no scope beyond reading events.

## Considered options

We rejected counting caps in the Hono or Finishing layer before the transaction, because two deliveries could both read the last free place. We rejected a counter column on the grant, because the rows that carry the grant's ID are the audit and a counter could drift from them. We rejected calendar-day caps in the Workspace zone for rolling windows, which need no zone and can't be gamed at midnight. We rejected accepting inside the proposal's own receipt transaction: the proposal's transaction would then hold the Workspace counter lock (its event flush) while taking the account lock, the reverse of the accept path's order. Accepting right after, under the account lock, gives the same outcome without a new lock order. We rejected letting a grant carry an override for `approval_required` brands, since the audited Owner or Admin override is a person's decision on one export with a reason, which a standing permission can't make. We rejected exposing grants on REST and MCP, since authority to act without a click is what a person grants in Narriflow.

## Consequences

The migration adds `StandingGrant` (with `ruleFingerprint` and the notice lease), `StandingGrantEvent`, `ReviewerOptOut`, `ContentPack.autopilotRuleId`, `GenerationFinishing.autopilotRuleId`, `SocialPost.standingGrantId` and `standingGrantRevision`, and `ReviewRound.standingGrantId`, `standingGrantRevision` and the unique `finishingId`; apply it before deploying. `Finishing`'s `delivery` gains `send_review_round`, which only Autopilot rules may use: inline REST and MCP requests are refused, because the grant is granted for a rule. A paused, revoked or expired grant leaves posts it already accepted scheduled and rounds already sent open; cancel them as any others.
