Every clip has one caption style. Choose it in Studio under **Captions**, on a Brand Profile's caption style, or by ID with `captionPreset` in the API and your assistants. Styles come in three kinds.

- **Dynamic styles** set each caption as a small block of rows. Narriflow picks one word per caption to show large, the hero, and sets the words around it smaller.
- **Motion styles** are drawn by a small program for each style. Shapes travel between words, the caption before and after the one you are on can stay on screen, and the style reads the transcript for pauses, lists, numbers, speech rate and who is speaking.
- **Classic styles** show each caption on one line in one font and size. Only the word being spoken changes, by colour, a highlight or a short animation.

## Dynamic styles

Dynamic styles are listed first in the gallery. Each one is a single click: Narriflow picks the hero, builds the rows, fits the block to the frame and keeps it clear of the top 10% and the bottom 18%, where platform buttons sit.

### Headline

`headline`. Small white rows above and below a heavy hero that fills most of the frame width, shaded from light mint at the top to green at the bottom. Words slide in; the hero rises into place. The shading belongs to Headline's own green: pick another hero colour, or an animation that grows or turns the hero, and the hero is one flat colour.

### Editorial

`editorial`. Small white sans-serif minor words, cream serif body words and a large lowercase serif hero. The rows above and below the hero sit off-centre, one to the left and one to the right. Words slide in; the hero rises into place out of a soft blur.

### Cutout

`cutout`. Each word sits on its own tilted tile in tall condensed capitals, orange on dark tiles, and words of one or two letters, like "I" and "it", dark on pink tiles. Cutout has no hero and shows up to five words across most of the frame width. Each word drops onto the frame.

<Frame caption="Cutout">

![Cutout: "AND I KEEP IT SHORT" on tilted tiles, "I" and "IT" on pink, the rest on dark](/docs/caption-styles/cutout.jpg)

</Frame>

### Marker

`marker`. A left-aligned lowercase stack beside the speaker: plain sans-serif rows around a heavy rounded hero in yellow or pink, alternating from caption to caption. Right-to-left languages such as Urdu align to the right edge instead. Words slide in; the hero rises into place, then a hand-drawn white squiggle draws itself under it.

<Frame caption="Marker">

![Marker: "week of" above a large yellow "content" with a white squiggle under it, "in" below, aligned to the left](/docs/caption-styles/marker.jpg)

</Frame>

### Stack

`stack`. A small, light lowercase row above a very large, heavy lowercase hero, all white. Three stars pop in just after the hero: a larger one at a top corner of the hero and two smaller ones at the opposite corners of the block. They take the accent colour.

<Frame caption="Stack">

![Stack: "how to edit" in light type above a large white "captions", with white stars at three corners](/docs/caption-styles/stack.jpg)

</Frame>

### Groove

`groove`. Serif capitals above and below a rounded, pink display hero with a capital first letter. Words slide in; the hero rises into place.

<Frame caption="Groove">

![Groove: "GET" above a large pink "Ready", with "W/ ME" below](/docs/caption-styles/groove.jpg)

</Frame>

### Arc

`arc`. Cream serif rows, each bent into an arch, around a larger line of the same face. Letters shrink a little toward the ends of each row, like a fish-eye lens. Words slide in; the hero rises into place.

Urdu, Arabic, Hindi, Thai, Hebrew, Chinese and Japanese captions stay straight in Arc: their letters join or have no spaces between words, and bending them letter by letter would break them apart.

## Motion styles

Motion styles come after Dynamic styles in the gallery, in the **Motion** group. A Motion style owns its typefaces, motion, shadows and outlines, so it has no animation picker. Everything on screen comes from the words spoken, plus counters and timestamps worked out from the speech. The studio preview, the exported video and the preset thumbnails are drawn from the same program, so the preview and the export match closely. Right-to-left captions such as Urdu run from the right, and scripts that join their letters are never split into letters. Captions keep clear of the frame edges.

Motion styles pick one word as the hero the way [Dynamic styles do](#how-the-hero-is-chosen); a caption with no clear hero shows without one.

### Lyrics

`lyrics`. The lyric view of a music app: the caption before and the one after stay on screen, soft and blurred, while the current one fills with colour word by word as it is spoken, and the hero takes the hero colour with a glow. A pause longer than 0.7 seconds shows three breathing dots where the next caption will appear.

<Frame caption="Lyrics">

![Lyrics: "with literally zero savings" with the hero "zero" in green, the caption before it dim above and the next one dim below](/docs/caption-styles/lyrics.jpg)

</Frame>

### Dialogue

`dialogue`. A conversation as a chat thread. The first speaker's captions appear on the left in grey bubbles and every other speaker's on the right in the accent colour; the words light up as they are spoken, the previous bubble steps back, and a pause of more than half a second before a caption shows a typing indicator first. It reads speakers and pauses.

<Frame caption="Dialogue">

![Dialogue: a grey bubble "So I quit my job today." on the left and a blue bubble "Wait, for real?" on the right](/docs/caption-styles/dialogue.jpg)

</Frame>

### Dossier

`dossier`. The rest of the sentence waits as black redaction bars on typed paper strips, and each bar wipes away the moment its word is spoken. The hero gets a red rubber stamp, and a pause before a word makes its bar blink like a cursor.

### Hollow

`hollow`. Tall condensed capitals wait as outlines and fill solid as each word is spoken. The hero fills in the hero colour and throws three outline echoes upward, which fold back into it as the caption leaves.

### Signal

`signal`. A terminal that glitches when it matters: the support words type onto one monospaced line behind a block cursor, then the hero lands with an RGB split, brackets lock onto it and a scan line reads it once. When the caption ends, everything collapses to a line.

### Track

`track`. Four corner brackets lock onto each word as it is spoken, like an object detector, and a small readout above the box shows the word's timestamp in the clip. The hero is larger and gets a KEY tag and a scan line. It reads the timing of each word.

### Bulletin

`bulletin`. A broadcast lower third: a coloured bar grows down the left side, square plates wipe in beside it, and each word rises inside its plate when it is spoken. It reads numbers: a number hero such as "312%" counts up from zero while a white rule draws under it.

<Frame caption="Bulletin">

### Ticker

`ticker`. A signal-yellow tape slams across the frame at the hero's spoken start and carries the hero as a scrolling marquee between four-point stars, with the support words riding above and below it. A caption with no hero gets a slim black tape with white words.

### Flap

`flap`. A split-flap departure board: the cards for a caption stand blank and each flaps over to its word on the beat it is spoken. The hero is an amber card that clatters twice, and at the end the cards flip back to blank one after another.

### Index

`index`. When the speaker counts points ("three mistakes ... first ... second ... so"), a numbered dial docks beside the caption card and its ring fills one segment per point as the list advances. It reads lists: the announced total, ordinals and next-words such as "first" or "then" that start a phrase, and the closing "so" or "that's" after a sentence ends. When the list closes the dial leaves and a plain bar takes its place.

<Frame caption="Index">

![Index: a dial reading 3 of 3 beside a dark caption card with the word "finally,"](/docs/caption-styles/index.jpg)

### Punchline

`punchline`. Ordinary lines pop in like stickers with a thick outline and a hard shadow. It reads pauses and sentence ends: a short line that ends a sentence after a real pause is a punchline, so three dots drumroll through the silence and the line slams onto a yellow starburst with a shock ring.

### Doodle

`doodle`. The caption is handwritten with a paint marker as it is spoken, each word wiped in left to right over its spoken time. The hero is tipped and gets a pen loop, three ticks and, when there is a lead-in, a hooked arrow pointing at it; a caption with no hero gets an underline.

### Velocity

`velocity`. Condensed capitals skid in on a forward lean, and the hero rides a slanted slab that slices in from the left. It reads speech rate: a calm caption barely tilts and has no speed lines, a fast one leans hard and streaks.

### Wordform

`wordform`. A strip of up to 44 bars runs under the caption on its own time axis: a bar where a word is spoken, taller for a weightier word and in the hero colour for the hero, and a flat dot where the speaker pauses. A playhead lights them as the caption plays, and the words fade in out of a small blur.

### Chrome

`chrome`. Y2K liquid chrome: the hero is a sheared chrome badge with a navy rim and a blue glow that slams in from large and blurred, catches a travelling glint and throws one sparkle off its corner. Support words are small, wide and quiet.

### Reveal

`reveal`. Every word rises out of an invisible slit, and the caption leaves the same way it came. The hero is a large italic serif set straight under the rest of the line.

<Frame caption="Reveal">

### Spine

`spine`. A Swiss poster. The hero runs up the left side of the frame in big red capitals, reading from bottom to top, and rises out of a hard edge with a dark print offset. Beside it sits a tidy column: the caption's number, a black rule that draws itself, and the other words on white plates that wipe in as they are spoken. A caption with no hero sets its plates larger across the frame.

### Boom

`boom`. A comic-book panel. The hero is a fat yellow word with a hot-pink 3D edge and a black rim. A two-layer starburst with halftone dots stamps in behind it and turns slowly, speed lines shoot out of it, and the other words pop in around it like lettering. A caption with no hero gets bigger lettering with a smaller burst behind its last word.

## How the hero is chosen

Narriflow scores the words of each caption from the transcript: numbers with their units (such as "20 dollars"), negations, stressed or drawn-out words, words before a pause and words at the end of a sentence score higher. Small words like "the", "of" and "is" never become the hero. Picking a hero uses no AI model and adds nothing to your credits.

When no word clearly stands out, the caption has no hero and shows as one row. A wrong hero looks worse than none.

Captions break between phrases where they can, and a caption never ends on a small word like "in" or "the" when the sentence goes on: that word starts the next caption instead. This applies to every style, classic ones too, and to subtitle files.

To change it, pause on the caption in Studio and choose another word in the **Captions** panel. You can also join the hero with the next word, turn emphasis off for that caption, or go back to the automatic pick. These choices belong to the clip; applying a style to all clips copies the style, not the choices.

## What you can change

The size slider and resizing on the video scale the whole block. Text colour sets the small words, and hero colour sets the hero. Styles that use a third colour, such as Cutout's pink tiles, Marker's second hero colour and Stack's stars, also show an accent colour. Fonts, animation, words per caption and the hero's size are under **Advanced**. So is **Curve**, for every dynamic style except Cutout: move it right to arch the rows, left to bow them, or to the middle for straight rows. Arc starts at 0.55. A curved hero is one flat colour, Marker's squiggle is left out, and animations that grow or turn words only fade and slide them. A caption with many long words, or in a script that cannot bend, stays straight. The underline and the stars need a moment to draw: a caption with less than 0.3 seconds left when its hero is spoken shows without them. Cutout has no hero, so it shows no hero colour, emphasis or hero settings; its text colour sets the letters on the dark tiles.

A Motion style offers fewer controls, because the style owns its motion: **Size**, **Position**, and the colours the style uses (Text, Hero and Accent), plus the emphasis row. The size slider and resizing on the video scale the whole style, and dragging moves it; the style keeps its own spacing as it moves. The preview in the panel plays the style's own sample.

Subtitle files (SRT and WebVTT) and editor timelines keep the transcript's words as they are. Dynamic and Motion styles change only how the burned-in captions look.

## Classic styles

Classic styles are grouped as Bold, Clean, Expressive and Effects. Use their IDs, such as `bold-pop` or `karaoke`, the same way. The [API reference](/docs/api/introduction) lists every ID.
