Every clip has one caption style. Choose it in Studio under **Captions**, on a Brand Profile's caption style, or by ID with `captionPreset` in the API and your assistants. Styles come in two kinds.

- **Dynamic styles** set each caption as a small block of rows. Narriflow picks one word per caption to show large, the hero, and sets the words around it smaller.
- **Classic styles** show each caption on one line in one font and size. Only the word being spoken changes, by colour, a highlight or a short animation.

## Dynamic styles

Dynamic styles are listed first in the gallery. Each one is a single click: Narriflow picks the hero, builds the rows, fits the block to the frame and keeps it clear of the top 10% and the bottom 18%, where platform buttons sit.

### Headline

`headline`. Small white rows above and below a heavy hero that fills most of the frame width, shaded from light mint at the top to green at the bottom. Words slide in; the hero rises into place. The shading belongs to Headline's own green: pick another hero colour, or an animation that grows or turns the hero, and the hero is one flat colour.

### Editorial

`editorial`. Small white sans-serif minor words, cream serif body words and a large lowercase serif hero. The rows above and below the hero sit off-centre, one to the left and one to the right. Words slide in; the hero rises into place out of a soft blur.

<Frame caption="Editorial">

![Editorial: "a big" above a large italic "problem", with "with" below](/docs/caption-styles/editorial.jpg)

</Frame>

### Cutout

`cutout`. Each word sits on its own tilted tile in tall condensed capitals, orange on dark tiles, and words of one or two letters, like "I" and "it", dark on pink tiles. Cutout has no hero and shows up to five words across most of the frame width. Each word drops onto the frame.

<Frame caption="Cutout">

![Cutout: "AND I KEEP IT SHORT" on tilted tiles, "I" and "IT" on pink, the rest on dark](/docs/caption-styles/cutout.jpg)

</Frame>

### Marker

`marker`. A left-aligned lowercase stack beside the speaker: plain sans-serif rows around a heavy rounded hero in yellow or pink, alternating from caption to caption. Right-to-left languages such as Urdu align to the right edge instead. Words slide in; the hero rises into place, then a hand-drawn white squiggle draws itself under it.

<Frame caption="Marker">

![Marker: "week of" above a large yellow "content" with a white squiggle under it, "in" below, aligned to the left](/docs/caption-styles/marker.jpg)

</Frame>

### Stack

`stack`. A small, light lowercase row above a very large, heavy lowercase hero, all white. Three stars pop in just after the hero: a larger one at a top corner of the hero and two smaller ones at the opposite corners of the block. They take the accent colour.

<Frame caption="Stack">

![Stack: "how to edit" in light type above a large white "captions", with white stars at three corners](/docs/caption-styles/stack.jpg)

</Frame>

### Groove

`groove`. Serif capitals above and below a rounded, pink display hero with a capital first letter. Words slide in; the hero rises into place.

<Frame caption="Groove">

![Groove: "GET" above a large pink "Ready", with "W/ ME" below](/docs/caption-styles/groove.jpg)

</Frame>

### Arc

`arc`. Cream serif rows, each bent into an arch, around a larger line of the same face. Letters shrink a little toward the ends of each row, like a fish-eye lens. Words slide in; the hero rises into place.

Urdu, Arabic, Hindi, Thai, Hebrew, Chinese and Japanese captions stay straight in Arc: their letters join or have no spaces between words, and bending them letter by letter would break them apart.

<Frame caption="Arc">

![Arc: "how to" arched above a large arched "curve", with "text" arched below](/docs/caption-styles/arc.jpg)

</Frame>

### Depth

`depth`. A huge lowercase white hero drawn behind the speaker, with small white words in front of them. Narriflow finds the person in each frame and, once per caption, moves the block up until the head covers at most the bottom third of the hero. If even the highest place the frame allows would hide more of it, for example when the head reaches the top of the frame, that caption's hero is drawn in front instead. Words slide in; the hero rises into place.

The speaker is found on Narriflow's own servers, with no AI service and nothing added to your credits. B-roll and inserted scenes cover the speaker, so a hero over them shows whole. If the speaker cannot be found, the hero is drawn in front.

</Frame>

## How the hero is chosen

Narriflow scores the words of each caption from the transcript: numbers with their units (such as "20 dollars"), negations, stressed or drawn-out words, words before a pause and words at the end of a sentence score higher. Small words like "the", "of" and "is" never become the hero. Picking a hero uses no AI model and adds nothing to your credits.

When no word clearly stands out, the caption has no hero and shows as one row. A wrong hero looks worse than none.

Captions break between phrases where they can, and a caption never ends on a small word like "in" or "the" when the sentence goes on: that word starts the next caption instead. This applies to every style, classic ones too, and to subtitle files.

To change it, pause on the caption in Studio and choose another word in the **Captions** panel. You can also join the hero with the next word, turn emphasis off for that caption, or go back to the automatic pick. These choices belong to the clip; applying a style to all clips copies the style, not the choices.

## What you can change

The size slider and resizing on the video scale the whole block. Text colour sets the small words, and hero colour sets the hero. Styles that use a third colour, such as Cutout's pink tiles, Marker's second hero colour and Stack's stars, also show an accent colour. Fonts, animation, words per caption and the hero's size are under **Advanced**. So is **Curve**, for every dynamic style except Cutout: move it right to arch the rows, left to bow them, or to the middle for straight rows. Arc starts at 0.55. A curved hero is one flat colour, Marker's squiggle is left out, and animations that grow or turn words only fade and slide them. A caption with many long words, or in a script that cannot bend, stays straight. The underline and the stars need a moment to draw: a caption with less than 0.3 seconds left when its hero is spoken shows without them. Cutout has no hero, so it shows no hero colour, emphasis or hero settings; its text colour sets the letters on the dark tiles. **Hero behind speaker**, under **Advanced**, draws the hero behind the person for the centred stacked styles (Headline, Editorial, Stack, Groove, Arc and Depth, where it starts on). Studio finds the speaker in the preview itself, so its placement can differ from the export by a few pixels.

Subtitle files (SRT and WebVTT) and editor timelines keep the transcript's words as they are. Dynamic styles change only how the burned-in captions look.

## Classic styles

Classic styles are grouped as Bold, Clean, Expressive and Effects. Use their IDs, such as `bold-pop` or `karaoke`, the same way. The [API reference](/docs/api/introduction) lists every ID.
